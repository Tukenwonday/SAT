Hydrogen Sulfide (H₂S) in Seed Germination

Hydrogen sulfide (H₂S) is typically toxic to mammals and plants at moderate concentrations. However, recent botanical studies conducted by researchers at Kyoto University investigated what effect minute, controlled exposures might have on early germination growth.


They treated bean, corn, wheat, and pea seeds with concentrations of H₂S ranging from 0.1 to 500 micromoles per liter. While toxic consequences were evident at high concentrations, low dosages demonstrated unexpected increases in enzymatic activity, stimulating α-amylase production essential for starch hydrolysis.
